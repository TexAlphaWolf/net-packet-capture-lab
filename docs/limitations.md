# Limitations

What pcaplab does not do, and where its findings can be wrong. The numbers behind the "not seen" cases are in the
[evaluation](evaluation.md), section 3.

## What it does not read

- **No TCP stream reassembly.** HTTP and TLS are recognised when the first segment of a message holds its start. A
  ClientHello split over several segments, or an HTTP header that starts in the middle of a stream, is not parsed.
- **No IP fragment reassembly.** The first fragment is decoded as far as it goes; later fragments are counted as "other".
- **Encrypted traffic stays encrypted.** QUIC is not parsed (its server name is invisible here), TLS 1.3 with Encrypted
  Client Hello hides the name, and DNS over HTTPS or TLS is not DNS to this tool: the DNS detectors see plain DNS only.
- **Link layers**: Ethernet (with up to two VLAN tags), Linux cooked v1 and v2, BSD/OpenBSD loopback and raw IP.
  802.11, PPP, Frame Relay and the like are counted as "could not be decoded".
- **Clear-text passwords**: HTTP Basic and FTP only. Not Telnet, POP3/IMAP/SMTP logins, HTTP forms, SNMP communities.
- **pcapng "simple packet" blocks have no time stamp**, so their time is 0.

## Where the detectors can be wrong

- **Port scans** are TCP only: UDP scans and ICMP sweeps are not covered. A scan slower than about one probe every four
  seconds (15 probes in 60 s) is invisible by design; a scan of fewer than 15 targets is below the threshold. A
  monitoring tool that checks 15 or more ports in a minute without sending data looks like a scan: use `--allow`.
  Connections that carry data are conversations, not probes, so a version scan that talks to the services is found
  through its first, probing phase only.
- **Beacons** need at least 8 connections and regular gaps: a jitter of 50% or more is not found. Anything periodic is
  found: monitoring, cron jobs, mail polling and update checks look the same as malware calling home, so the finding says
  "or of a monitor" and the `--allow` list exists. UDP beacons count each datagram flow, so a periodic UDP service on
  an unlisted port is reported.
- **DNS tunnels** are a heuristic on name length, count and randomness. Some benign names have the same look: security
  products and some CDNs query long random-looking names (`--ignore-domain` for those). A tunnel that uses short names or
  few queries is not found. The "domain" is the last two labels (three under `co.uk`-like suffixes), not the public
  suffix list.
- **NXDOMAIN storms** can also be a client with a broken search-domain list.
- **ARP conflicts** need both owners in the capture, and a legitimate change (a replaced machine, a DHCP lease moving
  an address) is reported as "moved" at medium severity. Capturing on one port of a switched network shows only part of
  the ARP traffic.
- **Retransmissions** are counted by sequence numbers: out-of-order segments count as repeats, and a capture taken on a
  sender with segmentation offload shows packets that never existed on the wire.

## About the numbers

The accuracy figures come from captures generated by `pcaplab.synth`, in which the attacks are injected at known places.
They show that the detectors do what they claim and where they stop; they do not show how often real networks look like
the generator. The only measurements on real traffic are the agreement with scapy on the captures of other people
(evaluation section 5), and those captures contain no attack, so no claim is made about detection rates on real attacks.
